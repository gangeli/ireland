## Family preferences (from notes and ratings)

Raise the score for:
- **Town access.** ≤15–20 min to a real town or city with a hospital, schools and services, e.g. Kilkenny. George: Grangehill 5, "very close to Kilkenny". Penalise ~30 min+: Forty Shades "hard to commute from, and get medical care".
- **Space for six.** About 180–280 m², 4+ bedrooms, large common areas, a good floor plan that flows. Penalise "small": Alderfern, Poppy House, Ballygowan, Tirernan, Jenkinstown. Converting the only playroom shrinks the common areas (Ballycarrigeen).
- **Bathrooms.** At least 3 for six, with a full bathroom on each sleeping floor. Penalise "only 1 bathroom for 4 bedrooms upstairs" (Ballyhast), "no bathrooms upstairs" (Ballycarrigeen) and "only two bathrooms" (Oak Tree, Tirernan).
- **Light and character.** Big windows, light, "character". Penalise "very few and small windows" (Stone Lodge) and "boring" architecture (Ballyhast).
- **Privacy.** Defined, ideally fenced front and back yard; isolated from neighbours.
- **Condition.** Turnkey or sound is a small plus, not a big one (see Fixer-uppers below).
- **Gabor's extras.** Single storey; walkable to a nice village, ideally one with a station (Friars Hill 5); charm (Poppy House 5); a sound single-storey on real land (Tombrack 4).

Lower the score for:
- **Work without payoff.** Lots of work on a house with no particular charm, layout or location to show for it, or structural doubts. Clomantagh 2 "lots of work without any particular promise of charm"; Killinardish 2; Leavalley "structural integrity is questionable".
- **Close agricultural or industrial neighbours.** Farmyards (Tirernan, Ballygowan, Ballyhast), a vehicle yard (Tombrack), a pet farm (Hillview), houses packed close (Ballinkillen).
- **Busy surroundings.** Right on a road (Tobinstown, Jenkinstown); beside the railway or a station (Old Station House).
- **Stairs.** Steep stairs or lots of stairs (Oak Tree, Old Station House, Ballinkillen). An awkward layout, such as rooms reached only through another room.
- **Upkeep.** Too much land or a big estate to maintain: Jenkinstown's 18.5 ac is "more of a problem than advantage unless it can be rented"; Woodbrook is "very expensive… maintaining it". Industrial-looking grounds.
- **No floor plan.** A light con, as an information gap; not a big penalty by itself.

Typical size of an adjustment: each clear strength or weakness moves the score about 0.25–0.5 from the 3.0 "sound but plain" baseline. Keep results spread between 2 and 4.5. Don't stack small items into huge swings.

## Fixer-uppers (Gabor, 27 Oct)
Don't over-weight turnkey condition. A fixer-upper that is otherwise great and comes in cheap should score on what it becomes, not on its state today. That means good bones, location, land, character, a layout that works or can be made to work, and the ground-floor bedroom.

Judge it on:
- **Total cost.** Price plus a rough, labelled renovation estimate, compared with the ~€500k budget. Friars Hill (€290k + €90–155k, Gabor 5) is the model.
- **The finished house.** Score it against the rest of this rubric.

Penalise condition only by how far it eats into the budget, plus a small deduction (about −0.25 to −0.5) for the hassle and risk. Use the full "lots of work" penalty only when there is little to show for the work: a plain house, a poor location or structural doubts.

Go up to about €650k for them. Score them on the rubric. Walkable town access with footpaths is a real plus. A town-edge road counts against only if it is genuinely busy at the gate.
